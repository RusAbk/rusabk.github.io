When I help

The work starts when operations become too manual to scale.

Most companies do not need a generic chatbot. They need cleaner processes, fewer handoffs, better data flow, and AI placed exactly where it changes the economics of work.

Managers do not see where time, leads, documents, and decisions get stuck.

Teams use chats, spreadsheets, and manual follow-ups instead of a stable process.

AI is discussed as a trend, but no one owns a concrete implementation plan.

Existing SaaS tools do not fit local workflows, languages, messengers, or team habits.

What I do

From process diagnosis to AI implementation.

I combine operator experience, technical implementation, and practical customer discovery. The goal is not to add AI everywhere, but to build workflows that teams actually use.

Process audit

Map the real workflow, expose bottlenecks, define ownership, and separate what should be automated from what should be redesigned first.

AI implementation

Build practical assistants, automations, document pipelines, internal knowledge tools, and API integrations that support daily work.

Operating playbooks

Turn informal knowledge into SOPs, onboarding flows, scripts, dashboards, and repeatable management routines.

Pilot to product path

Start with a focused implementation or consulting pilot, measure impact, and only then decide what should become software.

Method

A simple implementation loop.

Each engagement is designed to create visible operational improvement before committing to a heavy product build.

01

Diagnose

Interview the team, inspect tools, review handoffs, and identify the process constraints that actually cost time or money.

02

Design

Define the target workflow, success metrics, implementation scope, AI boundaries, and the smallest useful pilot.

03

Implement

Ship the automation, assistant, dashboard, playbook, or integration in a way the team can use without changing everything at once.

04

Improve

Measure adoption, fix edge cases, train the team, and decide what should be standardized, delegated, or productized next.
